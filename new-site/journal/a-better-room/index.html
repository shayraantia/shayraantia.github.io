THE ROOM / 3 MIN READ

Ask a question that opens the room.

A good introduction gives another person somewhere meaningful to begin.

SHAYRA ANTIA

Start with what they are making possible.

“What are you working on that you would like to move forward?” is a useful opening. It gives a founder room to talk about a company, a leader room to talk about a decision, and someone at the start room to talk about an idea.

You can follow with: “What would help?” The answer may be a different perspective, an introduction, or simply a sharper question. Listen before deciding which one you can offer.

Make your contribution specific.

A useful contribution can be small. A relevant example from your experience. A resource you have actually used. A thoughtful challenge that helps someone test an assumption.

If you think an introduction would help, ask both people before making it. Explain why you think the conversation could be useful, and leave each person room to decline.

Give the conversation a next step.

At the end, agree on something concrete when it is appropriate: a question to explore, a resource to send, or a time to continue. Let the other person help define the next step.

For a host, a good closing question is: “What are you leaving with that you want to act on?” It brings attention back to the ideas and relationships the room has made possible.

Bring a question of your own.

What are you trying to build? What decision is in front of you? What perspective would help?
